BEST HIKES IN BANFF

Pick the trail that fits your season, legs, shuttle plan, and weather window.

20-trail comparison for first-time planning

THE SHORT ANSWER

Best easy hike: Tunnel Mountain for a town-accessible summit or Johnston Canyon for waterfalls.

Best Lake Louise hike: Lake Agnes Tea House for most visitors; Plain of Six Glaciers for a longer glacier-view day.

Best fall hike: Larch Valley, if you have Moraine Lake access and trail conditions are appropriate.

Best hard day: Sentinel Pass, Cory Pass, Bourgeau Lake, or Healy Pass, only with fitness, daylight, gear, and current trail reports on your side.

Trail note: Distances and elevation below are planning ranges. Check Parks Canada trail conditions, closures, group restrictions, wildlife warnings, and Avalanche Canada before committing.

CHOOSE BY SEASON

May, June, winter, and larch season all require different expectations.

Winter

Stay low and check forecasts

Use Tunnel Mountain, Johnston Canyon, Bow River Trail, and town walks. Bring traction. Do not enter avalanche terrain casually.

May-June

Expect mixed conditions

Lower trails can be good while higher Lake Louise and Moraine Lake routes may hold snow, mud, ice, or closures.

July-August

Book access first

Trail choice often depends on whether you secured Lake Louise or Moraine Lake access and whether smoke or storms change the day.

September

Larches need discipline

Larch Valley and Sentinel Pass are famous for a reason, but crowds, weather, and Moraine Lake access require an early plan.

CHOOSE BY TRIP TYPE

The best Banff hike is the one that fits your day.

With kids

Keep distance honest

Try Bow River Trail, Johnston Canyon Lower Falls, Marble Canyon, lakeshore walks, or Tunnel Mountain for strong walkers.

No shuttle seat

Stay near Banff town

Use Tunnel Mountain, Bow Falls, Sundance Canyon, or lower-elevation viewpoints rather than gambling on Lake Louise parking.

Lake day

Pair hiking with logistics

If you already have Lake Louise shuttle access, Lake Agnes or Plain of Six Glaciers can anchor the day.

Hard hike

Earn the big views

Cory Pass, Sentinel Pass, Bourgeau Lake, Healy Pass, and long Lake Louise loops require real fitness and weather margin.

Canmore comparison

Do not mix geographies blindly

Canmore hikes can be excellent, but this page keeps Banff National Park plans separate so pass, parking, and shuttle logic stays clear.

First Banff trip

Choose one classic, not four

Pick Tunnel Mountain, Johnston Canyon, Lake Agnes, or Larch Valley depending on season and access, then leave recovery time.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Trail questions that change the plan.

What is the best easy hike in Banff?

Tunnel Mountain is the best easy summit near town. Johnston Canyon is the best easy waterfall walk if trail and access conditions are good.

What are the best Banff hikes in winter?

Use lower, maintained, town-adjacent routes such as Tunnel Mountain, Johnston Canyon, Bow River Trail, and short viewpoints. Check traction, closures, and avalanche forecasts first.

Do Banff hikes require a shuttle?

Town and Bow Valley hikes often do not. Lake Louise and Moraine Lake hikes need a parking, shuttle, transit, or licensed-operator plan in peak season.

What is the hardest hike in Banff for visitors?

Cory Pass, Bourgeau Lake, Healy Pass, Sentinel Pass, and long Lake Louise combinations are serious day hikes. Pick them only with fitness, daylight, current trail reports, and weather margin.
