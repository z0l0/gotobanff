THINGS TO DO IN BANFF

A decision guide for the lake days, hikes, viewpoints, drives, town time, and winter activities worth planning around.

Built for 2026 logistics and live search intent

THE SHORT ANSWER

First-time summer visitors: prioritize Lake Louise and Moraine Lake, Johnston Canyon, Lake Minnewanka, Bow Falls, and one summit or gondola viewpoint.

Winter visitors: swap alpine lake logistics for skiing, Johnston Canyon ice walks, hot springs, Banff town, and short scenic drives after checking roads.

Car-free visitors: Banff town, Roam routes, Parks Canada shuttles, and licensed operators can cover the major highlights without a rental car.

Source check: Moraine Lake, Lake Louise parking, and Parks Canada shuttle rules change by season. Confirm the exact date, fare, reservation window, and road status before you commit a day.

BEST PICKS BY AREA

Banff is easier when you plan in clusters.

Banff town

Use this for arrival day

Bow Falls, Surprise Corner, Banff Avenue, Cave and Basin, Vermilion Lakes, and the hot springs keep the first or last day flexible.

Lake Louise

Build one full lake day

Pair Lake Louise, Moraine Lake, and one realistic hike. Trying to add the Parkway or downtown Banff usually makes the day too thin.

Bow Valley Parkway

Slow down for viewpoints

Johnston Canyon, picnic stops, and wildlife-safe pullouts work best when you leave margin for closures, traffic, and weather.

Lake Minnewanka

Easy scenery without shuttle stress

Minnewanka, Two Jack Lake, and nearby viewpoints are useful when lake shuttle seats are gone or the alpine forecast is poor.

Icefields Parkway

Treat it as a travel day

The Parkway deserves daylight, fuel, food, and road-condition checks. It should not be a casual evening add-on.

Downtown backup

Keep one indoor-flex plan

Use restaurants, shops, visitor information, museums, and hot springs when smoke, rain, or winter roads change the plan.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Quick answers for common Banff planning decisions.

What is the number one thing to do in Banff?

For a first summer trip, make Lake Louise and Moraine Lake the priority because access requires advance planning and the scenery is the reason many visitors choose Banff.

What can you do in Banff without a car?

Stay in Banff town and combine walkable sights, Roam transit, Parks Canada shuttles, and licensed tours. It is strongest in summer, but winter visitors can still use town-based activities and resort shuttles.

What should I do if the weather is bad?

Move lower: Banff town, Bow Falls, Cave and Basin, hot springs, Lake Minnewanka viewpoints, cafes, visitor centres, and short drives after checking 511 Alberta.
